# Illustration Commission Brief — The Kamasutra Guide

Prepared for hiring a freelance illustrator (Fiverr, Upwork, or direct).
Hand this document to them as-is — it has everything needed to quote and
deliver the job. Not published anywhere; internal working brief only.

---

## 1. Project overview

**Product:** *The Kamasutra Guide* — a digital, self-contained app (single
HTML file) sold as a paid download, part of the After.9 collection. Buyers
are couples aged 21–40. The product is a browsable, filterable reference of
36 positions, each with a name, short tagline, and description.

**What's needed:** One illustration per position — **36 illustrations total**
— plus optionally 1 cover/hero illustration for the product listing itself
(37 total if included). Each illustration accompanies its position's text
entry inside the app and appears again on the Shopify product listing.

## 2. Content guidelines — read this section first

This is the part that matters most for a correct quote, and it's a hard
requirement, not a style suggestion:

- **Suggestive and artistic, never explicit.** No visible genitalia, no
  explicit nudity, no anatomically graphic depiction of sexual acts. Think
  the register of a tasteful yoga-pose diagram or a fashion-illustration
  couple sketch, not adult/pornographic art.
- **Stylized over literal.** Simplified, elegant forms — silhouette,
  continuous-line, or soft watercolor wash — that suggest body positioning
  and closeness without rendering realistic anatomy. Faces can be simplified
  or turned away/abstracted; this is about form and posture, not likeness.
- **Why this matters practically, not just tastefully:** this product needs
  to be listable on mainstream platforms (Shopify, potentially Etsy), which
  prohibit explicit/pornographic imagery. Illustrations that read as
  "explicit" rather than "suggestive" put the whole listing at risk of
  takedown. When in doubt on any single piece, the illustrator should err
  toward less explicit, not more.
- This mirrors the tone of the written content already in the product —
  descriptive and warm, never clinical or graphic — the art should match
  that register exactly.

## 3. Style direction

Recommend briefing the illustrator with **one** of these three directions
(pick whichever fits your taste — all three are common, findable reference
styles so the illustrator can match quickly):

1. **Continuous single-line couple sketch** — one unbroken line forming two
   entwined figures, minimalist, elegant, mono-line on a solid or subtly
   textured background. (Search reference: "one line drawing couple art.")
2. **Soft watercolor silhouette** — blush/wine-toned watercolor wash figures,
   soft edges, no hard outlines, romantic and painterly. (Search reference:
   "watercolor couple silhouette illustration.")
3. **Flat paper-cut / block silhouette** — solid-color silhouette forms,
   two-tone (e.g., cream figures on a wine background or vice versa), bold
   and graphic. (Search reference: "paper cut art couple silhouette.")

**Recommendation: option 1 (continuous line)**, since it reads as
intentionally artistic and abstracted at a glance — the style itself signals
"tasteful illustration," which helps with both the platform-policy concern
above and general buyer comfort.

## 5. Technical specs

- **Format:** SVG preferred (scales cleanly at any size, tiny file size,
  fits the product's "single self-contained HTML file" delivery model with
  no external image hosting needed). If the illustrator can't produce clean
  SVG, high-resolution PNG with a transparent background is the fallback —
  at least 1200×1200px per piece.
- **Aspect ratio:** roughly square (1:1) or a gentle portrait (4:5) — needs
  to sit cleanly inside a mobile-width card layout.
- **File naming:** match the position ids exactly so they drop straight
  into the app: `f1.svg` through `f12.svg` (Foundations), `d1.svg` through
  `d12.svg` (Deepening), `u1.svg` through `u12.svg` (Uncharted). Cover
  illustration, if commissioned: `cover.svg`.
- **Delivery:** all files in one archive, one consistent style throughout —
  consistency across all 36 matters more than any single piece being
  exceptional.

## 6. Licensing / usage rights

Require **full commercial usage rights, exclusive and unlimited** — the
illustrations will be sold as part of a paid digital product to an unlimited
number of buyers. Do not accept a "personal use" or "limited print run"
stock-illustrator license; confirm explicitly before hiring that the
illustrator sells full commercial/resale rights, and get it in writing as
part of the contract or gig terms.

## 7. Budget & timeline guidance

Rough market range for 36 illustrations in one consistent simple style from
a freelance marketplace (Fiverr/Upwork), as of typical current rates:
**$150–$500 USD total**, depending on the illustrator's experience level and
whether it's a simple single-line style (cheaper) or detailed watercolor
(pricier). Get 2–3 quotes before committing. Expect a **1–3 week turnaround**
for a batch this size from an established freelancer; request 3–5 sample
pieces first (a mix of easy and hard poses) before greenlighting the full 36,
so style/consistency issues surface early rather than after full delivery.

## 8. Revision process

Ask for one full revision round included in the quoted price. Review
against the content guidelines in Section 2 on every single piece before
accepting delivery — a batch job means one illustrator's early or late
pieces can drift in explicitness or style; check all 36, not a sample.

## Appendix — full position list (all 36 entries, grouped by chapter)

Give the illustrator this full appendix. Each entry's **Line** is the
short tagline (closest thing to a one-sentence visual brief); the
**Description** gives fuller context on the physical positioning if the
illustrator wants more to work from.

### Chapter 1 — Foundations

**f1. The Anchor _(a classic face-to-face)_**
- Tags: Energy gentle · Flexibility low · Experience beginner
- Line: Grounded and close, eye contact the whole way through.
- Description: Lying face to face, one partner settles between the other's legs, weight supported on forearms so nothing feels rushed or heavy. Hips stay level and close, with the pace set by whoever's underneath.

**f2. Slow Tide _(spooning)_**
- Tags: Energy gentle · Flexibility low · Experience beginner
- Line: Side by side, barely any effort, built for unhurried mornings.
- Description: Both partners lie on their sides facing the same direction, the one behind curled in close. Movement stays small and slow — this one rewards patience over pace.

**f3. The Cradle**
- Tags: Energy gentle · Flexibility low · Experience beginner
- Line: Seated closeness, arms wrapped all the way around.
- Description: One partner sits back against pillows or a headboard with knees bent; the other settles into their lap facing them, legs wrapped around their waist. Hands stay free for holding, not balancing.

**f4. Close Quarters**
- Tags: Energy gentle · Flexibility low · Experience beginner
- Line: Seated, foreheads together, nowhere to look but at each other.
- Description: Both partners sit cross-legged facing one another, knees overlapping, torsos pressed close. It's less about depth and more about proximity — foreheads touching, hands on each other's faces.

**f5. The Hollow**
- Tags: Energy gentle · Flexibility low · Experience beginner
- Line: A lower, flatter spoon for total relaxation.
- Description: Similar to Slow Tide but lying flatter, almost prone, with the partner behind draped fully along their back. Nothing here requires any real strength from either person.

**f6. Evening Light**
- Tags: Energy gentle · Flexibility low · Experience beginner
- Line: Face to face, legs woven together, hands free.
- Description: Both partners lie on their sides at the same height, facing each other, one leg draped over the other's hip so the lower bodies interlock. Faces stay close enough to talk quietly throughout.

**f7. The Nest**
- Tags: Energy gentle · Flexibility low · Experience beginner
- Line: One partner reclined and supported, the other half-draped alongside.
- Description: One partner lies back against a stack of pillows; the other settles half on top, half beside them, weight distributed rather than centered. It reads more like an embrace than a position.

**f8. Quiet Harbor**
- Tags: Energy gentle · Flexibility low · Experience beginner
- Line: The simplest version — flat, close, hands clasped.
- Description: Both partners lie flat, face to face, hands laced together at chest height. No elevation, no bracing — just closeness and steady eye contact.

**f9. The Threshold**
- Tags: Energy gentle · Flexibility low · Experience beginner
- Line: Standing, upright, weight resting on a wall or doorway.
- Description: Both partners stand facing each other, one leaning back lightly against a wall or door frame for support. Little movement is needed — most of the connection comes from closeness and stillness.

**f10. First Light**
- Tags: Energy gentle · Flexibility low · Experience beginner
- Line: A gentle variation with one leg raised for a different angle.
- Description: Built on The Anchor's foundation, with the receiving partner raising one leg slightly, resting it on the other's shoulder or hip. The added angle is subtle, not acrobatic.

**f11. The Warm Wrap**
- Tags: Energy gentle · Flexibility low · Experience beginner
- Line: Fully entwined, blanket-close, made for staying still.
- Description: Both partners lie on their sides, wrapped fully around one another — arms, legs, everything interlaced. It's closer to an extended embrace than a position with a distinct shape.

**f12. Steady Ground**
- Tags: Energy gentle · Flexibility low · Experience beginner
- Line: Kneeling, upright, torsos pressed close.
- Description: Both partners kneel facing each other on a bed or soft surface, torsos pressed together, hands resting on shoulders or upper back for balance. The upright angle is a small step up from lying positions.


### Chapter 2 — Deepening

**d1. The Bridge**
- Tags: Energy moderate · Flexibility medium · Experience intermediate
- Line: Hips elevated on pillows, a different angle without much effort.
- Description: One partner reclines with hips raised on a stack of pillows or the edge of a bed; the other kneels facing them. The elevation noticeably changes the angle while either partner can still adjust the pace easily.

**d2. Rising Tide**
- Tags: Energy moderate · Flexibility medium · Experience intermediate
- Line: One seated at the edge, one kneeling or standing to meet them.
- Description: One partner sits at the edge of a bed or sturdy chair; the other kneels or stands facing them. The height difference gives both partners a freer range of motion than lying flat.

**d3. The Lotus Circle _(also known as Yab-Yum)_**
- Tags: Energy moderate · Flexibility medium · Experience intermediate
- Line: Seated, wrapped around each other, chest to chest.
- Description: One partner sits upright, legs crossed or extended; the other settles into their lap facing them, legs wrapped around their waist. Both stay upright and close, hands free to hold each other the entire time.

**d4. Open Window**
- Tags: Energy moderate · Flexibility medium · Experience intermediate
- Line: Legs raised and open, supported by an attentive partner.
- Description: One partner lies back with knees drawn toward their chest, legs open; the other kneels close, using their arms to support the raised legs rather than leaving them to hold the position alone.

**d5. The Turn**
- Tags: Energy moderate · Flexibility medium · Experience intermediate
- Line: Side by side, partly rotated, one leg draped high.
- Description: Both partners lie on their sides at a slight angle to one another rather than fully facing; the top leg drapes over the other's hip for a rotated, in-between angle.

**d6. Second Wind**
- Tags: Energy moderate · Flexibility medium · Experience intermediate
- Line: Rear-facing, kneeling, torsos close rather than distant.
- Description: One partner kneels with torso upright; the other kneels close behind, chest nearly against their back rather than leaning far away. The closeness keeps it more intimate than the angle alone suggests.

**d7. The Perch**
- Tags: Energy moderate · Flexibility medium · Experience intermediate
- Line: One seated on a raised surface, the other standing close.
- Description: One partner sits on a counter, dresser, or sturdy table at a height that lines up comfortably; the other stands facing them. The elevation does the work that flexibility would otherwise need to.

**d8. Shifting Sands**
- Tags: Energy moderate · Flexibility medium · Experience intermediate
- Line: Reclining with hips turned slightly to one side.
- Description: One partner reclines with hips rotated a little to one side rather than square to their partner, torso following partway. It's a small change from a flat position but shifts the angle meaningfully.

**d9. The Weave**
- Tags: Energy moderate · Flexibility medium · Experience intermediate
- Line: Legs crossed and interlaced, side-facing.
- Description: Both partners lie on their sides, facing each other, with legs crossed over one another in a scissor-like weave. It asks for a bit more coordination than fully parallel positions but stays low-effort overall.

**d10. Rising Vine**
- Tags: Energy moderate · Flexibility medium · Experience intermediate
- Line: One kneeling upright, the other leaning back over their thighs.
- Description: One partner kneels upright; the other reclines back over their thighs, supported by the kneeling partner's arms rather than their own core. It's a trust-forward position more than a strength-forward one.

**d11. The Overlook**
- Tags: Energy moderate · Flexibility medium · Experience intermediate
- Line: Leaning forward on support, a moderate-angle approach from behind.
- Description: One partner leans forward, supported on their forearms against a bed or cushioned surface; the other kneels close behind at a moderate, comfortable angle rather than a steep one.

**d12. Balanced Arch**
- Tags: Energy moderate · Flexibility medium · Experience intermediate
- Line: A slight backbend, cushioned and supported.
- Description: One partner reclines into a gentle backward arch, supported by a stack of pillows behind the shoulders and lower back; the other kneels close. The arch is shallow, not a full bend.


### Chapter 3 — Uncharted

**u1. The Suspension Bridge**
- Tags: Energy intense · Flexibility high · Experience advanced
- Line: Weight-bearing, braced against sturdy furniture.
- Description: One partner braces against a sturdy piece of furniture — a bed frame, a solid chair — while the other supports a portion of their weight from behind. This one leans on strength and stability more than any Foundations or Deepening entry.

**u2. The Standing Embrace**
- Tags: Energy intense · Flexibility high · Experience advanced
- Line: Fully standing, full weight support, built for strong arms.
- Description: One partner is lifted and supported entirely by the other while standing, legs wrapped around their partner's waist. This asks a lot of the supporting partner's strength and balance and isn't one to hold for long stretches.

**u3. The Wheel**
- Tags: Energy intense · Flexibility high · Experience advanced
- Line: A deep backbend, carefully supported throughout.
- Description: One partner arches back deeply — hands or forearms reaching toward the floor or a low surface — while the other supports the movement closely. This is a flexibility-forward position that needs real warmup beforehand.

**u4. Full Bloom**
- Tags: Energy intense · Flexibility high · Experience advanced
- Line: Legs raised high, deep flexibility required.
- Description: One partner lies back with both legs raised high and open, well past a Deepening-level stretch; the other kneels close, taking on more of the supporting role than usual. Deep hip and hamstring flexibility matters here.

**u5. The Climb**
- Tags: Energy intense · Flexibility high · Experience advanced
- Line: One partner elevated on furniture, height-coordinated.
- Description: One partner sits or reclines on a raised, stable surface — a bed at counter height, for instance — while the other stands to meet them. Getting the height right takes some trial and error the first time.

**u6. Reverse Arch**
- Tags: Energy intense · Flexibility high · Experience advanced
- Line: Kneeling with a deep arch, hands reaching back.
- Description: One partner kneels upright and arches back deeply enough to reach toward their ankles, held steady by the other's hands at the hips. It's one of the more visually dramatic entries and one of the more demanding ones.

**u7. The Counterweight**
- Tags: Energy intense · Flexibility high · Experience advanced
- Line: Standing, one leg lifted high, full partner support.
- Description: Both partners stand; one lifts a single leg high while the other supports both the lifted leg and a good portion of their balance. It's closer to a partner-assisted stretch than a resting position.

**u8. Edge of Balance**
- Tags: Energy intense · Flexibility high · Experience advanced
- Line: Seated on a furniture edge, legs wrapped high, core-engaged.
- Description: One partner sits at the very edge of a bed or sturdy chair with legs wrapped high around the other, who stands or kneels close. It asks for real core engagement from the seated partner to hold the position.

**u9. The Deep Fold**
- Tags: Energy intense · Flexibility high · Experience advanced
- Line: Legs folded toward the chest, face to face, high flexibility.
- Description: One partner folds their legs in close toward their chest while lying on their back, going noticeably deeper than Open Window; the other leans in close, face to face throughout. This is one of the more flexibility-demanding entries in the guide.

**u10. Skyward**
- Tags: Energy intense · Flexibility high · Experience advanced
- Line: Hips elevated high on a stacked support, standing partner.
- Description: One partner's hips rest high on a stacked support — several pillows or a raised piece of furniture — while the other stands facing them. The height does most of the work; the flexibility asked of the elevated partner is what makes it advanced.

**u11. The Spiral**
- Tags: Energy intense · Flexibility high · Experience advanced
- Line: A rotated, twisted torso position — flexibility over strength.
- Description: One partner lies with hips facing one direction and shoulders twisted to face the other, a deeper rotation than The Turn. It asks for spinal flexibility more than raw strength.

**u12. Full Embrace Standing**
- Tags: Energy intense · Flexibility high · Experience advanced
- Line: A full standing carry — the most demanding entry in the guide.
- Description: One partner carries the other's full body weight in a standing embrace, legs fully wrapped, no furniture involved. This is the most physically demanding entry in the collection and isn't meant to be held for long.
